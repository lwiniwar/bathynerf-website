Project abstract

In the BathyNeRF project, we investigate the use of Neural Radiance Fields for bathymetric reconstructions. As such, we want to include modelling of the refractive air/water surface in the radiance models. The project aims at investigating different approaches with respect to their ability to accuractly reconstruct below-water geometry from above-water imagery, typically acquired by uncrewed aerial vehicles (UAVs). One of the core challenges is exploiting the specific properties of topographic data and a-priori knowledge to aid in the modelling process. In an application example, we want to investigate the capability of NeRFs to represent below-water vegetation as a penetrable and volumetric object.

Recent publications from our team at the ISPRS Congress in Toronto

Investigating the Potential of SfM, MVS, and Monocular Depth Estimation for Water Surface Reconstruction


Anatol Günthner, Markus Brezovsky, Frederik Schulte, Lukas Winiwarter, Gottfried Mandlburger, Boris Jutzi

Reconstructing the water surface in refractive domains such as rivers and lakes is challenging, since light bending at the air-water interface alters the apparent geometry and breaks the straight-ray assumption of conventional image-based 3D reconstruction.

Int. Arch. Photogramm. Remote Sens. Spatial Inf. Sci., XI-2, 585–592, https://doi.org/10.5194/isprs-annals-XI-2-2026-585-2026, 2026.
